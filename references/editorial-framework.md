# Editorial framework

## Build the argument before the treatment

Write the opening question and supported answer first. Each section should remove a specific obstacle to that answer. A list, a causal chain, and a clue-and-reveal structure are different argument shapes; choose one because it fits the evidence, not because it is visually fashionable.

## Assign each medium a job

- **Footage and photographs:** setting, action, people, and lived context.
- **Documents and quotations:** attributable reporting or primary-source wording.
- **Diagrams:** relationships, flows, constraints, and mechanisms.
- **Charts:** comparable measured values with units, dates, populations, and scales.
- **Interviews:** attributed testimony.

Mark every asset as proof, context, analogy, or reconstruction. Do not let a trophy, product image, stock shot, or decorative interface stand in for a claim it cannot establish.

## Design the semantic operation

Before animation, name the actor, resource, unit, relation, constraint, and consequence. Then make the smallest before-and-after state that communicates the change. Use reordering, transfer, comparison, accumulation, or replacement only when the claim actually involves that operation.

## Use continuity deliberately

Keep an actor, badge, legend, or comparison axis on screen when the audience needs it to understand a relationship. Release it when a new subject or evidence class needs the frame. A hard cut can be clearer than an artificial morph.

## Preserve provenance

Retain source, date, scope, uncertainty, and hypothesis or prediction status in the claim map. A source crop supports attribution but does not independently prove every assertion inside it. Make designed quotations and illustrative documents visibly distinct from authentic records.

## Treat audio and pace separately

Timing should follow meaning, narration phrasing, reading time, density, and contrast. Do not derive universal beat counts from a small sample. Review narration, music, effects, silence, and phrase-to-image alignment through actual playback before approving sound.
