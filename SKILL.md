# Editorial analysis for sports and business documentaries

This is an independent editorial-analysis skill. It is not affiliated with Athletic Interest and does not reproduce that channel's media, branding, scripts, research corpus, or production assets.

## Start with evidence and scope

1. Define the question, stakes, named actors, and what would count as evidence before choosing a visual treatment.
2. Keep metadata, transcript reading, sampled-frame inspection, continuous visual review, direct audio review, and full audiovisual review as separate states. Do not call extraction, captions, or contact sheets “watching.”
3. Record source, date, rights status, exact subject, and whether an asset is proof, context, analogy, or reconstruction. A visually persuasive asset is not necessarily evidence.
4. Label conclusions `measured`, `observed`, `inferred`, or `provisional`; preserve uncertainty and counterexamples.

See [research protocol](references/research-protocol.md) and [audiovisual review limits](references/audiovisual-review.md).

## Develop the editorial argument

1. Build a claim map: opening question, supported answer, evidence, uncertainty, and the question handed to the next section.
2. Build an asset board with provenance, clearance, narrative job, and production gaps. Do not use one generic image as evidence for several different claims.
3. For each sequence, specify `argument -> visual evidence -> operation -> consequence`. Use the medium that serves the claim: footage for lived action, documents for attribution, diagrams for relationships, charts for measured change, and interviews for attributed testimony.
4. Specify a mechanism before animating it: actors, units, constraints, movement, and consequence. Animation is not explanation.
5. Keep visual continuity only while it aids comprehension. A deliberate cut, held frame, full-screen source, or change of medium is valid when it clarifies the argument.
6. Let palette, type, texture, and framing serve the sequence rather than imitating a fixed channel look. Use original project identity.
7. Treat audio as its own deliverable: narration clarity, clip sound, music, silence, and transitions require direct review.

See [editorial framework](references/editorial-framework.md) and [production specification](references/production-specification.md).

## Implement and review

- Choose a toolchain after the edit and asset plan; browser, NLE, compositor, canvas, and hybrid workflows are implementation choices, not styles.
- For HTML work, make time deterministic, support reverse seeking, wait for assets and layout, and validate encoded output rather than DOM state.
- Before a full render, review a storyboard or animatic against the claim map, available assets, and a counterexample to the dominant treatment.
- Keep technical, factual, editorial, and audio verdicts independent. An unreviewed gate remains unreviewed.

See [quality review](references/quality-review.md) and [render reliability](references/render-reliability.md).
